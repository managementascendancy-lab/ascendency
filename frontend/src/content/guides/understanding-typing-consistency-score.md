## Two different questions, not two names for the same thing

Typing consistency vs accuracy trips people up because both sound like they're measuring "how good" a run was — but they answer completely different questions. Accuracy asks how many characters you got right. Consistency asks how steady your pace was while you were typing them. A run can be highly accurate and still wildly inconsistent, or fairly consistent while making occasional mistakes. They're independent metrics on purpose.

## It's measured against your own average, not a fixed pace

A common misconception is that consistency means capping your speed to avoid variance — it doesn't. The score measures how steady your pace is relative to *your own* average during that run. A sustained 90 WPM the whole way through scores just as well as a sustained 40 WPM. The goal isn't typing slowly on purpose; it's avoiding the burst-then-crash pattern regardless of what pace you're bursting from.

## Why it's a separate gate, not folded into accuracy

If consistency were just averaged into accuracy, a burst-then-crash run could still post a decent composite score by getting the crashed-through words right eventually. Keeping consistency as its own independent threshold on every [hero classification](/guides/hero-classification-system-explained) closes that gap — a hero tier means you can sustain the pace, not just eventually get there.

## What causes inconsistency in practice

The most common cause is hitting an unfamiliar word or punctuation mark mid-flow and hesitating before continuing — the hands know most of the passage but stall on the part they don't. The fix isn't slowing down everywhere; it's specifically drilling the patterns that cause the stall, so the hesitation stops happening rather than just becoming less frequent.

## Check your own pattern

Your simulation history on your [Ascendant Console](/profile) shows your consistency score alongside WPM and accuracy for every run. If your WPM looks strong but consistency lags behind it, that's usually the clearest sign you're bursting rather than sustaining — worth a few practice runs focused purely on steady pace before pushing top speed again.
