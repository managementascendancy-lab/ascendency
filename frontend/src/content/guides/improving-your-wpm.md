## Speed is a side effect, not a goal

The biggest mistake people make when trying to type faster is chasing speed directly. WPM is a byproduct of two things: how confidently your fingers know where the keys are, and how little your brain has to consciously translate a word into a sequence of keystrokes. Chase those two things and the number takes care of itself.

## 1. Slow down before you speed up

If you're making more than 2-3 errors per 100 characters, you're already typing too fast for your current skill level. Every error costs you far more time than typing slightly slower would have — you have to notice it, stop, backspace, and re-type. Run a few simulations at a pace where your accuracy sits above 97%, then gradually push the pace up.

## 2. Fix your weakest fingers, not your whole hand

Almost everyone has one or two fingers doing more than their fair share of work — usually the index fingers — while the ring and pinky fingers lag behind. Pay attention to which characters make you hesitate. If it's always `q`, `p`, `z`, or punctuation, that's a pinky problem, and it's worth deliberately practicing words that lean on those keys rather than avoiding them.

## 3. Type in chunks, not letters

Slow typists process one letter at a time. Fast typists recognize common letter clusters — "tion", "ing", "the", "and" — as single motor patterns. This comes from repetition, not effort. The more volume you type, the more these patterns get burned in automatically.

## 4. Consistency beats bursts

A run where you hit 90 WPM for three seconds and 40 WPM for the rest isn't actually a 90 WPM run — and Ascendancy's [consistency metric](/guides/understanding-hero-classification) will call that out. Smooth, sustained pace is both faster on average and a better sign of real skill than spiky bursts followed by recovery.

## 5. Warm up before a serious attempt

Just like any physical skill, your hands benefit from a short warm-up. Run one or two easy simulations before attempting a personal best — cold fingers make more mistakes, and mistakes are the real WPM killer.

## Put it into practice

Head to the [Training Simulator](/simulator) and run a 60-second session focused purely on accuracy first. Once you're consistently above 97%, gradually push your pace on the next few attempts. Progress compounds — small, steady gains beat one-off lucky bursts.
