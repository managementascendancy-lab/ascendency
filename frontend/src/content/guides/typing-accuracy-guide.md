## The math behind the classification system

Every [hero classification](/ascendancy) on Ascendancy has independent minimum thresholds for WPM, accuracy and consistency — you don't get to average your way past a requirement. SOVEREIGN needs 96%+ accuracy no matter how fast you type. A blazing 140 WPM run at 85% accuracy won't classify anywhere near the top, because accuracy is the hard gate, not a bonus stat.

## Errors cost more time than they seem to

On most typing interfaces, a single mistake costs you the time to type the wrong character, notice it, and correct it — often 3-4 keystrokes of overhead for a 1-character error. Typing 10% faster while making 3x the errors is almost always a net loss in real elapsed time, even before it affects your accuracy score directly.

## Your hands already know what to do — get out of the way

Most accuracy problems aren't a knowledge problem, they're an interference problem. Your fingers usually know the correct motion; anxiety about speed causes you to rush the motion before it completes, or to glance at the keyboard and lose your place in the text. Trust the motion, don't out-think it mid-keystroke.

## Practice at "boring" speed on purpose

It feels unproductive to deliberately type slower than you're capable of, but it's one of the highest-leverage things you can do. Running simulations where your only goal is zero errors — regardless of how slow that makes you — retrains the underlying motor pattern to be accurate by default, not accurate by effort.

## Watch for fatigue-driven accuracy drops

Accuracy tends to degrade in the last third of longer simulations as finger fatigue sets in. If your accuracy consistently drops off in 120-second runs, that's a sign to build up endurance gradually with shorter, high-accuracy sessions before attempting longer ones.

## The takeaway

If you only optimize one metric on Ascendancy, optimize accuracy first. It's the foundation the other two — consistency and, eventually, speed — are built on. Check your current numbers on your [Ascendant Console](/profile) and see which one is actually holding your classification back.

## Worked example: the 140 WPM run, fully classified

The article opens with "a blazing 140 WPM run at 85% accuracy won't classify anywhere near the top" — here's exactly why. Checking that run against the [full tier table](/guides/hero-classification-system-explained): 140 WPM alone clears every tier's speed requirement, all the way up to SOVEREIGN's 130 WPM. But 85% accuracy only just clears VANGUARD's 85% minimum — it fails PHANTOM's 88%, TITAN's 90%, and every tier above that. So despite outrunning SOVEREIGN's WPM requirement by 10 WPM, this run classifies at VANGUARD at best, entirely because of one metric. A much slower 50 WPM run at 96% accuracy and reasonable consistency would comfortably outclassify it — proof that accuracy, not raw speed, is the harder-earned and more decisive metric of the three.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a minimum accuracy I should aim for before pushing speed?
The article's own guidance is to practice runs where your only goal is zero errors, regardless of pace — that trains accuracy as a default rather than something you have to concentrate on. Once that feels automatic, gradually reintroducing speed tends to hold up much better than chasing speed first.

### Why does a small accuracy drop hurt my classification more than a small speed drop?
Because accuracy thresholds tend to climb faster than WPM thresholds across the [tier table](/guides/hero-classification-system-explained) — AEGIS, for example, demands 95% accuracy at a comparatively modest 62 WPM. Missing an accuracy threshold by a percentage point or two is a harder gap to close than missing a WPM threshold by the same relative margin.

### Does correcting an error as I type still count against me?
Every correction still costs the overhead of noticing the mistake, backspacing and retyping — roughly 3-4 keystrokes per error — even if the text you submit ends up correct. That overhead is exactly why fewer errors in the first place beats fast typing with fast corrections; see [the drills that actually move your WPM](/guides/improving-your-wpm) for more on that tradeoff.

### Why does my accuracy drop specifically toward the end of longer runs?
That's fatigue, and it's common — see "Watch for fatigue-driven accuracy drops" above. If it happens consistently in 120-second runs, build up endurance gradually with shorter, high-accuracy sessions rather than repeatedly grinding through the same long runs at the same fatigue point.

### Is accuracy the same thing as consistency?
No — they measure different things entirely. Accuracy is how many characters you got right; consistency is how steady your pace was while typing them. A run can be highly accurate and still wildly inconsistent, or the reverse.
