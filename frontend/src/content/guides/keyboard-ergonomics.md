## Speed built on bad posture doesn't last

It's possible to grind out impressive WPM numbers with terrible wrist position and hunched shoulders for a while — but repetitive strain doesn't announce itself until it's already a problem. If you're training seriously, your setup matters as much as your technique.

## Wrist position

Keep your wrists straight, not bent up, down, or to either side. A bent wrist under repetitive motion is the single biggest contributor to strain injuries. If your desk is too high, your wrists will naturally cock upward to reach the keyboard — that's a desk problem, not a typing problem, and worth fixing before you fix your technique.

## Home row discipline

Your fingers should rest on the home row (`ASDF` / `JKL;`) between bursts of typing, not hover over wherever they last landed. Most keyboards have small raised bumps on the F and J keys specifically so you can find home row without looking. Use them — this is what lets you keep your eyes on the screen instead of the keyboard.

## Shoulders and elbows

Elbows should sit close to your body at roughly a 90-degree angle, not flared out or stretched forward. Flared elbows force your wrists into worse angles to compensate, even if you don't notice it happening in the moment.

## Take real breaks

Repetitive strain builds up from volume over time, not from any single session. If you're running back-to-back simulations for an extended session, stand up and shake your hands out every 15-20 minutes. It costs you almost nothing and meaningfully reduces long-term risk.

## Keyboard choice matters less than you think

A lot of typing advice fixates on mechanical switches, split keyboards, or specific layouts. These can help at the margins, but good technique on a mediocre keyboard will always beat bad technique on an expensive one. Fix your fundamentals first — upgrade hardware later if you still want to.

## Bring it into your training

Good ergonomics isn't separate from performance — it's what lets you sustain consistency over a full [simulation](/simulator) instead of degrading in the back half from discomfort. Set up your space once, and every session after benefits from it.
