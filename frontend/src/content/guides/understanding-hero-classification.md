## Ten tiers, three independent gates

Ascendancy classifies every simulation into one of ten heroes, from NOVA at the start to SOVEREIGN at the apex. Each tier has its own minimum thresholds for WPM, accuracy and consistency, and — critically — you need to clear **all three** to unlock it. There's no averaging your way past a weak metric with a strong one.

## Why it's built this way

A typing performance metric that only measured speed would reward reckless typing. One that only measured accuracy would reward typing so slowly that mistakes are trivially avoidable. Consistency alone would reward someone who types the same mediocre pace the whole way through. Requiring all three together is what makes a classification actually mean something.

## Reading your own progress

Your [Ascendant Console](/profile) shows your current classification along with a progress ring toward the next tier. That ring is driven by whichever of the three metrics you're furthest from clearing — if your WPM already qualifies for NEXUS but your consistency doesn't, consistency is your bottleneck, not speed.

## Classification uses your best run, not an average

Your leaderboard score and current hero are both driven by your single best verified simulation, not a rolling average. One excellent run outweighs ten mediocre ones. This means it's worth occasionally running a simulation purely focused on a personal best rather than always training incrementally.

## The archive is deterministic

Every threshold in the [Classification Archive](/ascendancy) is fixed and public — there's no hidden curve or moving target. If you clear NEXUS's thresholds today, you'll clear them next week too, regardless of how the rest of the network is performing. Your classification is about your performance against a fixed bar, not a ranking against other people (that's what the separate leaderboard is for).

## Common misconception: consistency isn't about being slow

A lot of people assume "consistency" means capping their own speed to avoid variance. It actually measures how steady your pace is relative to your own average — a sustained 90 WPM the whole way through scores just as well on consistency as a sustained 40 WPM. The goal isn't to type slowly on purpose, it's to avoid the burst-then-crash pattern.
